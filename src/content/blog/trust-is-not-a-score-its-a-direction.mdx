Trust is not a dashboard metric. It is the long, quiet consequence of keeping promises while the system is under load. A score can go up while the experience goes down. The direction is what matters.

## Consistency beats spectacle

People do not remember your best day. They remember your worst day. When the system fails, how do you behave? The answer becomes the story, and the story becomes the trust.

## The cost of hidden failure

Failure is inevitable, but silent failure is a decision. If you allow the system to hide its errors, you are also hiding its debt. Trust erodes in the gap between what is said and what is endured.

## Direction is measurable

You can see direction in the cadence of updates, the speed of repair, and the honesty of postmortems. The question is not "are we trusted". The question is "are we more trustworthy than last quarter".
